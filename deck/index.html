Recommendations06 / 08

Three moves, in priority order

01

Attack retention, not just the funnel

Stand up a lifecycle program — post-purchase onboarding and a day-30/60 win-back — aimed squarely at the month-0 → month-1 cliff. Track repeat-rate at 30/60/90 days as the north-star metric.

02

Grow returning-revenue share on purpose

It drifted to ~15% on its own — make it a target. Test loyalty and replenishment nudges on first-time buyers in the highest-margin categories, and measure incremental repeat purchases.

03

Instrument before pricing & shipping bets

Add order-level shipping_fee and promo flags, define the launch date, and reserve a holdout — turning a “free shipping over $100” launch into a measurable difference-in-differences test.

The through-line: a few points of retention compound faster than the same dollars spent re-acquiring customers the business already had.

Method07 / 08

How this was measured

Scope & window

91 complete months, 2019-02 → 2026-08. The current partial month is excluded so growth and trend lines are not distorted by an unfinished month.

Definitions

Completed sale = status Complete and not returned. Revenue = Σ sale_price; margin uses inventory cost. New vs returning is keyed on each customer’s first completed order.

Churn is deliberately simple

The 90-day churn is calendar-anchored and binary, reported only for fully-observed months. In production I’d anchor the window to each customer’s last order.

Free shipping is a scenario, not data

thelook has no shipping-fee field, so that task builds the difference-in-differences frame you’d use to measure such a policy — rather than reporting a fabricated effect.
